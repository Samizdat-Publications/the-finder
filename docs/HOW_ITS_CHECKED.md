physics or history it rests on, the data it uses, the automated test that proves it, and the honest error
bars and caveats. It becomes a page on the site (`/checked/`).

table is generated from the test output (`docs/checks/results.json`) so it cannot drift from the code._

## Assumptions and honest caveats

- **The story's epoch is a projection.** The alien's catalogue is the real ATNF catalogue and real star
  data, projected 44,100 years forward with documented models: spin-down with braking indices (Crab
  2.342 and Vela 1.7 measured long-term; unmeasured ones drawn from stated ranges), seeded timing noise on the
  measured spin-down rate (per pulsar from Lyne et al. 2010 where measured, rms half the
  peak-to-peak; otherwise 0.425%, half the median of Lyne's 17), observer-frame Doppler, light time and the Shklovskii change for
  an observer at Gliese 445, linear stellar motion, and Voyager 1's asymptote. Old pulsars' tiny glitches
  are neglected in the estimate; left in the truth they move the combined date by about -6 years
  against a combined uncertainty of about 110 years (a one-sided bias, measured). Today's values are
  measurements; the future ones are physics applied to them.
- **The neighbour twist uses Gaia DR3:** the Sun is 1.024 pc from Gliese 445 at the epoch. Older
  pre-Gaia figures (about 1.06 pc) differ by 0.036 pc, many times Gaia DR3's 0.0016 pc uncertainty. About half the stars within 30 pc lack radial
  velocities; 19 of them could come closer than the Sun only with radial speeds of at least 64 km/s.
- **The map alone cannot tell the Sun from Gliese 445:** in 1971 Gliese 445 was 5 pc from the Sun and
  lies inside the map's 95% region too. The Trail decides it.
- **NASA's flyby figures are inconsistent** (1.6 or 1.7 light-years, "40,000 years", "year 40,272"). We
  use Bailer-Jones and Farnocchia (2019, Gaia DR2), rechecked with Gaia DR3.
- **"Ultra-pure" uranium** is assumed isotopically pure U-238, as NASA's wording implies; the two-isotope
  measurement would reveal otherwise.
- **The uranium spot's position** is not on the cover diagram; ours is a documented choice.
- **The colour order** of the 20 triples cannot be proven from the record alone; shown as "probable".
- **The side break** of the record is unconfirmed; shown as an inference.
- **The pulsar map's own limits:** B1240-64's period was known to 3 figures; the 1970 distances are off by
  up to about 6 times; the height ticks lack a sign.
- **The map's periods date from about 1971**, six years before launch.
- **The decoded pictures** are as the 1977 tape gives them: noisy, with wow and flutter and droop.
- **Five text pages** (President Carter's statement and members of Congress) were added to the record at
  the last minute and are not on the master tape we decode.

**Notes and honest error bars**

- `arrival.fuelAge`: The thorium and radium ratios cannot separate "U-234 from the start" from the plutonium chain (they differ by the plutonium mean life, about 127 years); that wrong answer is refused by the made-not-mined finding and the plutonium family in the spectrum, not by the clock.
- `arrival.heat`: Generous to every wrong answer: the lowest heat (radon escaping), perfectly black cylinders, beryllium's specific heat for the whole mass, beryllium burning as the fuel. "Away from every star" is the shortest straight-line time back to any catalogued star within 30 pc, with stars lacking a radial velocity allowed any speed up to the Galactic escape speed.
- `arrival.purpose`: A judgement deduction, not a numbers one. The dish facing the Sun is the scene's composition (the attitude is not computable); the angle between the Sun and the path back is computed. The surface roughness is a scan observation, not a measured number.
- `chart.galaxy`: Pulsars are placed at their catalogue distances (the alien's own measurements); distances from dispersion measures can be off by a factor of 1.3 or more. Brightness and size are display choices (galaxyModel.ts).
- `clocks.rtg.coverage`: Half-life systematic, 1 sigma, propagated per nuclide: LNHB U-234 131.0 y, Th-230 -38.7 y, Pu-238 0.04 y, total 136.6 y; with the Cheng et al. 2013 set (1 sigma = half their 2 sigma) 29.2 y. Cross-check only: the LNHB and Cheng central ages differ by -0.1 y, because on the atom ratio the U-234 and Th-230 shifts nearly cancel (the research's 103 y is for the activity ratio).
- `clocks.rtg.heat`: Range assumptions: high = radon retained, so the Ra-226 progeny (Rn-222 to Pb-206, 28.01 MeV per decay, NNDC; gammas counted, an upper bound by about 1.7 MeV) add their heat; low = radon escapes. About 1.5 W comes from Pu-239, which rests on the Cassini split of the non-238 plutonium (an assumption). Alpha decays deposit their full Q value; Pu-241 its mean beta energy. Activities at 44,000 y: U-234 2.55 TBq, Th-230 0.90 TBq, Ra-226 0.86 TBq, Pu-239 1.76 TBq, others 0.037 TBq.
- `clocks.rtg.inventory`: PuO2 molar mass 270.29 g/mol. Non-238 plutonium (20 at.%) split as Cassini fuel: atom fractions Pu-239 0.1699, Pu-240 0.0238, Pu-241 0.00473, Pu-242 0.00154 (an assumption). The Pu-239, Pu-240, Pu-242, U-235, U-236 and Np-237 amounts below rest on that Cassini split and on no uranium impurity in the fuel. At 44,000 y per spacecraft: Pu-239 0.768 kg, Pu-240 3.67 g, Pu-242 23.0 g, Pu-241 none, U-235 1.92 kg, U-236 0.373 kg, Np-237 74 g.
- `clocks.rtg.parent`: The 1.7e-4 readout (U-238 grown from Pu-242) depends on the Cassini isotopic split assumed for the non-238 plutonium and on no uranium impurity in the fuel; the deduction does not, as the contamination case shows.
- `clocks.rtg.wrongChain`: Th-230/U-234 and Ra-226/Th-230 alone barely separate the two chains (both ages land near 44,000 to 48,000 years); the decisive evidence is the missing U-238, which natural uranium would carry at 18,200 times the U-234.
- `clocks.uranium.coverage44k`: Pure U-238 start assumed; uranium and thorium separated before counting; same efficiency for every line. Half-life systematic 1 sigma (LNHB): 108 y (U-234 108 y, U-238 1.8e-4 y); x 1.96 for 95%.
- `clocks.uranium.coverage49`: Pure U-238 start assumed; uranium and thorium separated before counting; same efficiency for every line. Half-life systematic 1 sigma (LNHB): 0.12 y (U-234 0.12 y, U-238 2.1e-10 y); x 1.96 for 95%.
- `clocks.uranium.ratios`: Assumes isotopically pure U-238 at plating (NASA: "ultra-pure"); 9.62 Bq from NASA's 0.00026 microcurie.
- `cover.svg.binaries`: The disputed small label above the first raster lines (read as 10000 by one source, 1/10/11 by another) is drawn as plain marks and not asserted. 24 groups checked.
- `cover.svg.map`: This tests the map against its own data, not against the 1977 photo: the photo differs from the Pioneer artwork by up to 3 degrees on some lines and 2 to 6 % in length (cover.md 1f); see the alignment report in build/cover/landmarks.json.

Consistency: glitch-corrected Vela lies 0.01 yr from the Crab, which needs no correction. The research value 1971.77 added back a glitch (#2, MJD 41192) that precedes the date it solved for and omitted the January 2000 glitch at PEPOCH.

- `decoder.circle`: Ellipse fitted to the ring centre line by algebraic least squares (Halir and Flusser, as scikit-image EllipseModel).
- `decoder.frames`: Node decode time 114 ms per frame including WAV and PNG reading.

The shear's origin is unresolved: the 1977 equipment, the artwork or its copy, the gallery scans, or our decode. Our own circle is a diagonal ellipse (0.9934 at 137 deg; shear +0.0066 if the artwork is round), opposite in sign, and the gallery circle (0.986 at 136.9 deg) sits on the same diagonal.
- `decoder.ref.circle`: Not an independent geometry test: see decoder.ref.aspectIndependent. The fitted ellipse is not a perfect circle: minor/major 0.993 with a diagonal major axis, a shear no row count removes.
- `decoder.ref.frameStarts`: Barry's table is validation only, never a seed. L041: his start lies inside the start-of-picture comb.
- `decoder.ref.linePeriod`: The period drifts from 3197.4 to about 3193.9 samples along the reel, the same on both channels (a tape transport effect). Only the first frame is compared, because the research's value is for the opening frames; the median (8.3204 ms) and the drift are reported, not gated.
- `decoder.ref.slant`: A self-test of the meter the browser tests reuse, not an independent measurement.
- `decoder.ref.ssim96`: Same decoder and geometry at both rates; the 96 kHz decode is seeded only by the slice index.
- `decoder.slant`: The browser decodes 96 kHz slices; the reference measured at 384 kHz. The Python reference run once on the same 96 kHz slice gave the same comb values as this decoder (8.379 without, 0.585 with the offset), so the difference from the 384 kHz values is the lower rate, not the port.
- `epoch.craft`: Linear coast along the asymptote from the 2026 Horizons state; the Sun's remaining pull shifts the craft by about 20 AU over 150,000 AU (epoch.ts).
- `epoch.discovery`: Straight-line motion of both bodies from the Gaia DR3 astrometry of Gliese 445 and the Bailer-Jones and Farnocchia asymptote, with no Galactic potential, as in the research DR3 recheck.
