# M6: Portrait, Our Frame, and a game with no dead ends

_Written by the lead in build session 2 (2026-10-16), at the M5 gate. Effort: high._

**Goal:** the story's end and the game around it. Portrait turns the codex, until now a ledger, into the
Finder's portrait of the senders: where they live, when they sent it, what they look like, their world and
their voices, assembled from the player's own confirmed deductions, ending with the Finder looking up at
the warm-white star in its own sky. Our Frame steps out of the fiction to today: where the two Voyagers
are right now, counting live; Voyager 1 crossing one light-day from the Sun on 2026-11-18; which
instruments still run; the slow future; the map measured six years before launch; the words etched in the
record's run-out groove. Around them: the whole game finishable by anyone, with hints, saves, a chapter
select after finishing, settings and accessibility.

**Done when (PLAN.md M6):** a full-playthrough bot from a fresh save to the epilogue's last screen;
no dead ends (every deduction reachable, every seed, every save); hints, saves, settings and accessibility
checked; critics at the gate; a preview link to Stewart.

## What the player does

**8. Portrait (about 5 minutes).** Unlocks when the deductions it draws on are confirmed (gating below).
The codex's entries rearrange into one page in the Finder's script and notation: the senders' star (the
warm-white star, the nearest of the Finder's neighbours), when they sent the machine (the clocks agreeing:
the fuel, the spot, the map's reframed age, the trail), how they look (the two figures, their height in
reaches, their numerals), their world (the third, its numbers), their voices (many; their music's
doubling and twelve steps). No new facts: every line is a confirmed deduction's own computed result, and
each links back to its How it's checked row. One deduction ties it together (candidate: "Who sent it?",
answered by assembling the portrait from the codex on the instrument, not from a list). It ends in the
encounter scene's sky, turned to the warm-white star, which the Finder now knows by name in its own
script: the moment the critic asked for instead of a ledger.

**9. Our Frame (about 5 minutes).** After Portrait. The voice changes from the Finder's to ours (plainly
marked as leaving the fiction): Voyager 1 and 2 now, their distances counting up live from the shipped
JPL tables (`epilogue.ephemeris`, `epilogue.today`); Voyager 1 one light-day from the Sun on
2026-11-18 10:16:18 UTC (`epilogue.lightDay`); which instruments still run (sourced, dated); the slow
future (the real Gliese 445 pass at about 44,100 years, the game's own premise); the map's periods
measured about six years before launch, so the map is older than the machine; the run-out inscription
"To the makers of music - all worlds, all times."; the credits and the How it's checked page. No
deduction here; a quiet ending with the record playing.

## The game around them
- **No dead ends:** every chapter finishable from every validated seed (`data/seeds.json` re-verified
  through Portrait); an old save always loads into a playable state; a chapter select after finishing.
- **Hints:** the three tiers exist; check every node has all three and that the third tier always lets a
  stuck player through on the instrument (a test per node: the answer tier names what to do on the
  instrument, and the bot can follow it).
- **Settings and accessibility:** keyboard play for every instrument (no deduction needs a mouse);
  screen-reader labels on controls and results; captions for every sound; reduced motion everywhere
  (moments as stills); contrast checked on every new surface; the human gloss everywhere a number shows;
  a "best on a bigger screen" note on phones without breaking.
- **From the critics, carried:** Arrival as evidence selection (pick the scan readings that support or
  refute each idea before choosing), if it fits M6; otherwise M7.
