## 3. The pulsars at the story epoch (`pulsarEpoch.ts`)
Build the alien's catalogue: every one of the 4,503 ATNF pulsars as Gliese 445 would measure it at the discovery epoch T. This is a simulation of the future, labelled as a projection; every model input below is a documented input, not a tuned number.
- **Spin (SSB frame):** from the catalogue (P0, P1 at PEPOCH, with P0, P1 derived from F0, F1 when needed), spin forward to the emission epoch with a braking index n: P^(n-1) = P0^(n-1) + (n-1) P0^(n-2) Pdot0 t (n = 1: exponential), Pdot = Pdot0 (P/P0)^(2-n). Test the closed form against a numerical integration of dP/dt = k P^(2-n) within 1e-9 relative. Pulsars without P1 keep P and are flagged `noSpinDown`.
- **Braking index (truth):** Crab 2.342 (Lyne et al. 2015, long-term average including glitches), Vela 1.7 (Espinoza et al. 2017, long-term), all others drawn per realization: old pulsars (characteristic age above 300 kyr) from U[2, 4] (a broad range around the magnetic-dipole value 3; clocks.md 3b shows the age changes by at most about 1.1% per unit n for these), young ones (B1727-47 and any other below 300 kyr) from U[0.9, 3.2] (the range of measured young-pulsar braking indices; cite the compilation you use, for example Espinoza et al. 2017 Table or Hamil et al. 2015). A "nominal" realization uses n = 3 for every unmeasured pulsar.
- **Light time:** the observer at position X (pc from the SSB) receives at time T what an SSB observer would receive at T + (X . n_hat) / c, n_hat the unit vector to the pulsar. Up to about 3.5 yr at 1.06 pc.
- **Doppler (the dominant effect):** observed period P_obs = P_SSB / (gamma (1 + beta . n_hat)), beta = V_obs / c, V_obs the observer's velocity relative to the SSB (about 110 km/s for Gliese 445). Plus the parallax term: the observer's line of sight differs from the SSB's by delta_n_hat = n_hat_obs - n_hat_SSB (up to about 0.2 deg), which projects the pulsar's transverse velocity (known from proper motion and distance; zero and flagged when either is missing) onto the line of sight: a factor (1 + v_t . delta_n_hat / c). Show in a comment that Pdot is unchanged by a constant Doppler factor to first order.
- **Shklovskii change:** the catalogue Pdot contains the SSB-frame Shklovskii term P v_t^2 / (c d). The observer sees the transverse velocity relative to itself, so Pdot_obs = Pdot_SSB + P (|v_t,rel|^2 - |v_t|^2) / (c d) (zero and flagged without proper motion or distance). This matters at about 1% for B0950+08 and B2016+28: compute and report it.
- **Timing noise:** the measured Pdot is Pdot_obs (1 + sigma_tn z), z standard normal from a seeded stream, sigma_tn = 0.01 (clocks.md 3b point 3: spin-down-rate switching of 0.84% to 2.53% peak to peak, "about 1% of the mean value", Lyne et al. 2010; one sigma of 1% is conservative against an rms of about half the peak-to-peak). Period measurements themselves are exact at this precision (comment why).
- **Positions:** RA/Dec moved by proper motion over T minus the position epoch, then seen from the observer (parallax from X when the distance is known). Galactic l, b recomputed. Distances unchanged (radial velocities of pulsars are unknown; comment the effect: second order for bearings).
- Glitches of the old pulsars: tiny (at most 2.6e-9 each); estimate their cumulative effect over 44 kyr for B1642-03 from its observed rate (7 glitches in about 40 years) and show it is below the timing-noise term; then neglect it, with that comment. Young pulsars' glitches are inside their long-term braking indices.
- API sketch: `projectCatalogue(cat: PulsarCatalogue, observer: Observer, opts: { seed: string; nominal?: boolean }): AlienPulsar[]`, each `AlienPulsar` carrying the observed quantities (`pObs`, `pdotMeas`, `lDeg`, `bDeg`, `distKpc`, flags) and a separate `truth` block (`pSsbAtEmission`, `n`, `emissionYear`, the Doppler and Shklovskii terms) that only tests read.

## 4. Identification (`pulsars.ts`): each map line has exactly one consistent match
The alien's procedure (PLAN.md chapter 2), with tolerances taken from documented reasons, never tuned to pass:
- **Direction:** the candidate's galactic longitude (as the observer sees it) within 5 deg of the line's map angle: 3 deg for the map's own accuracy (measured maximum 2.7 deg, cover.md section 2) plus 2 deg for proper motion and parallax since the map was made (the largest among the 14 at the epoch: about 1.8 deg for B1727-47 plus about 0.25 deg of parallax; compute and report the real maximum).
- **Age consistency:** the elapsed time implied by the candidate, t = (P^(n-1) - P_map^(n-1)) / ((n-1) P^(n-2) Pdot), evaluated over n in [0.9, 4], over a Doppler allowance of +/- 1e-3 on P (relative speeds up to 300 km/s), and over the map period's precision as the identifier knows it, must overlap the age window. The window and precision differ by who is identifying:
  - Today (`decimalYear` 2026.79, SSB frame, no Doppler allowance): window [0, 59] yr (no pulsar was known before 1967, so no map of pulsar periods is older); map precision from Sagan, Sagan and Drake 1972 (9 significant figures, 3 for B1240-64, about 6 for B1727-47; human knowledge, fine for this test).
  - At the epoch (the alien's catalogue): window [t_RTG,lo, 2 t_RTG,hi], where [t_RTG,lo, t_RTG,hi] is the 95% interval of the RTG Th-230/U-234 clock at the epoch from `clocks.ts` (one seeded measurement at 1% precision; the map is older than the craft's fuel and, generously, no more than twice as old); map precision 1 LSB (the binary hides Sagan's warning about B1240-64).
- Candidates are searched among all 4,503 pulsars. Test (ids `identify.today`, `identify.epoch`): every line has exactly one candidate and it is the right J name. Record the number of direction-only candidates per line too (it shows how much the period does). If any line has zero or several candidates under these documented tolerances, do NOT adjust them: record the failure, list the competing candidates with their numbers, and report it first.

## 5. Dating at the story epoch (`pulsars.ts` or `pulsarEpoch.ts`)
- `datePulsar(entry, line, opts)`: elapsed time since the map epoch from the alien's measured P and Pdot and the map period (HYDROGEN_HZ), with an honest 95% interval from a seeded Monte Carlo (at least 2,000 draws) over: the braking-index prior (old U[2,4]; young U[0.9,3.2]; Crab and Vela use the same young prior: the alien does not know their long-term n), the timing noise (the true long-term Pdot is Pdot_meas / (1 + sigma_tn z)), the map period's 1-LSB precision, and, when reframing, the observer's velocity and position uncertainty (`observer.velCov`, `posCov`) and each pulsar's distance uncertainty for the parallax and Shklovskii terms (parallax error when PX is used; otherwise +/- 30% for DM distances, a documented typical YMW16 accuracy).
- **Without the reframe** (the alien's raw Gliese 445 frame): the old pulsars' errors follow -2 tau_c (V_obs . n_hat) / c (clocks.md 3b point 4: 400 yr per km/s for B2016+28). Test in a realization with timing noise, light time, parallax and Shklovskii switched off and n_true = n_assumed: each old pulsar's error matches the prediction within 2%; then with everything on, report the scatter (expect thousands of years). Id `dating.epoch.noReframe`.
- **With the reframe** (after The Trail identifies the Sun, the alien moves its measured periods and Pdots into the Sun's rest frame using its measured Sun-relative velocity and position, that is the home star's state drawn from N(truth, covariance), and corrects light time, parallax and Shklovskii): combine the old pulsars (characteristic age above 300 kyr) with a robust rule (for example iteratively drop the pulsar whose estimate is most inconsistent with the weighted mean of the rest when it is more than 4 sigma away, then the weighted mean and its interval); the young pulsars are consistency checks only.
- The truth for each pulsar is the time from its own implied epoch today (section 2, raw; glitch-corrected for Vela and B1727-47) to its emission epoch at T; the combined truth is the same weighted combination of the individual truths. For B1240-64 the truth is T minus 1970.0 and its map period is wrong, so it must come out as an outlier.
- Tests (ids `dating.epoch.reframe`, `dating.epoch.coverage`, `dating.epoch.outlier`, `dating.epoch.young`):
  - Over 300 seeded realizations (truth draws and measurement draws from independent forks), the combined 95% interval contains the combined truth in 92% to 98% of them (300 trials: binomial s.e. 1.26%, so this is about 2.4 sigma; widen only by the trial-count argument, never to pass). Report the median interval half-width (expect a few hundred years).
  - B1240-64 is dropped by the robust rule in at least 99% of realizations, with its offset +3,400 +/- 300 yr (clocks.md 3b point 6).
  - Each young pulsar's interval contains its truth in at least 90% of realizations; report the typical widths (tens of percent, braking-index dominated).
  - Determinism: the same seed gives identical results.
- **The alternative the alien could find:** `fitAgeAndVelocity(entries)`: from the old pulsars alone (no star catalogue), fit one shared elapsed time and the observer's 3D velocity relative to the map's frame by nonlinear least squares (ageing scales with Pdot, Doppler with P cos theta, clocks.md 3b point 4), with a covariance from the timing-noise model. Test: the fitted velocity's 95% region contains the true Gliese 445 velocity relative to the SSB in at least 90% of 200 realizations; record the velocity precision per axis and the age interval. Id `dating.epoch.velocityFit`. (If the vertical component is unconstrained by these low-latitude pulsars, say so with numbers; that is a finding, not a failure.)

## 6. Triangulation (`triangulation.ts`)
- In the galactic plane (galactic Cartesian, from `astro.ts`), unknowns: the map origin (x, y) and a common rotation alpha of the map; data: the 14 map angles; residuals: wrapped angle differences between each map angle plus alpha and the bearing from the origin to the pulsar's in-plane position at the map epoch. Use the catalogue positions (l, b, distance) moved back by proper motion over the elapsed time (today: about 55 yr; at the epoch: the dated elapsed time); radial motion unknown (second order for bearings from near the Sun; comment it).
- sigma_theta: estimated from the fit's own residuals (report it; expect about 1.5 deg). Robust loss (Huber or Student-t) so one bad line cannot drag the fit.
- The map's line lengths: weak, heavy-tailed constraints (log of map length times a free scale versus log of the modelled in-plane distance, Cauchy loss) with a free scale. Show what changes when they are dropped.
- Region: Monte Carlo (at least 2,000 seeded draws) over the angle noise (sigma_theta) and the pulsar distances (parallax errors where PX is used; +/- 30% for DM distances), refitting each time; the 95% region as an ellipse from the draws.
- Tests (ids `triangulation.today`, `triangulation.epoch`, `triangulation.mirror`, `triangulation.wrongId`):
  - Today and at the epoch, the 95% region contains the Sun (the origin of the SSB frame at the map epoch). Report its semi-axes in pc and the number of `stars.json` stars whose in-plane positions (at the map epoch) fall inside it (a lower bound: the catalogue is incomplete for faint stars), next to Sagan's design claim (about 20 pc, "1 in 10^3 stars", cover.md section 3). Also report whether Gliese 445's position at the epoch, moved back to the map epoch in the Sun's frame, lies inside or outside the region.
  - The mirrored reading (all map angles negated, as if seen from the other galactic pole) fits far worse: its residual rms at least 5 times the correct one (report both).
  - A deliberately wrong identification (swap the two nearest pulsars' catalogue entries, B0950+08 and B1929+10): the residuals rise far above the map's 3 deg (rms at least 3 times the correct fit; report).
