## What the player does (the slice, concretely)

**0. Arrival (about 10 min).** A three.js scene: Voyager 1 (NASA's glTF, free to use) tiny and cold in
the vessel's lamps, under the real sky at the epoch seen from the craft (computed from `stars.json`
and `sky.json` with `epoch.ts`; the Sun a bright yellow star at magnitude -0.8). Three scans:
structure (the dish and booms highlight: a codex entry "built to send across distance, and to sense"),
heat (three cylinders faintly warm, 4.9 to 8.7 W in all, from `rtg.ts`), isotopes (a mass-spectrometer
readout of the RTG material at 44,100 years from `rtgState`, with seeded measurement noise). The decay
clock workbench: choose a chain hypothesis (Pu-238 to U-234 to Th-230; "natural uranium"; others),
see predicted ratio curves against the measured points; the right chain gives about 44,100 years with a
computed interval; "natural uranium" fails for lack of U-238 (D-A14). Codex: "They make elements."

**1. The Key (about 10 min).** The cover in the alien viewer under a raking light the player moves (an
exact SVG rebuild of the cover, every binary string from `cover.json`). Mark the two kinds of strokes
(bar across the line of writing = 1, dash along it = 0); read "1" between the two drawn states of
hydrogen; choose which hydrogen transition is the unit from an atlas (21 cm hyperfine, Lyman-alpha,
Balmer-alpha, others): every choice converts the cover's numbers live in a units panel, and only the
spin-flip gives sensible values (3.6 s per turn, not microseconds; times, not lengths: the turn read as a
length would be a million kilometres). Codex: the key.

**5. The Player (about 10 min).** The cover's record drawing, the cover's numbers now readable; a
turntable workbench (the M2 engine): set seconds per turn, the needle start, the direction; a microscope
view of the groove shows which way the spiral runs. Drop the needle: the first sound off the disc
(side 1 starts with the picture signal, then the greetings). Wrong speed: pitch and tempo shift exactly;
wrong direction: backward; a needle off the music: silence or run-out clicks.

**6. The Pictures, to the circle (about 15 min).** An oscilloscope of the signal beside the cover's
picture diagrams; a free-running decoder (sync lock is something the player discovers): tune the line
time (the cover's 8.34 ms), find the sync pulses and lock to them, the staggered interlace, polarity,
rows (aspect) until the calibration circle comes out round (`src/dsp/ellipse.ts` measures it live);
then the next frames develop line by line at record speed. Codex: "They see."

## Interface and presentation (v1)
- **Alien shell:** the instrument frame (deep indigo, cyan-green, violet; gold only for the human
  artifact and what has been decoded from it), glyph labels with a quiet English gloss, alien units in
  world with the human gloss setting (D-A07), a workbench layout (one instrument at a time, the codex
  always reachable), keyboard and mouse.
- **Codex v1:** entries unlock as deductions are confirmed; each shows the alien's notation, the gloss,
  and a "How it's checked" link to the matching row.
- **Deduction graph** (`src/game/deductions.ts`): nodes, prerequisites (hub after The Key, D-A06), states
  (unknown, hypothesis, confirmed), evidence; every state change through the bus; saves in
  localStorage (versioned; a "start over" control).
- **Hints:** a minimal three-tier hint (nudge, method, answer) per slice deduction, shown only when asked
  (full hint pass in M6).
- **Sound v1:** synthesized interface sounds (hums, plucks, a harmonic swell when the circle resolves),
  the stylus touch; the record's own audio stays clean. ElevenLabs textures only if they clearly help
  (log spend).
- **Encounter scene v1:** NASA's Voyager glTF cleaned and compressed (asset-smith, gltf-transform), a
  simple gold record on the bus, lamp lighting, the computed star field; WebGL2, one animation loop,
  clamped dt.
