## The instrument and the eight checkers (deduce on the instrument; a wrong answer shows its computed result)
1. `pictures.album` (answer `{ developed }`): the rest of the picture side plays through the decoder with the player's settings (the record-speed reveal, or "develop all" at speed); the album fills with every decoded frame as a thumbnail. 2. `pictures.colour` (answer `{ frames: [a, b, c] }`): the player picks three consecutive frames in the album and stacks them as three primaries on a colour mixer; OK when they are one of the record's triples (`colourRole`) and the three are the same scene (a computed similarity between the three far above that of neighbouring frames that are not a triple). The order is shown as "probable: red, green, blue" with the evidence the research names, computed (for example the mean levels of the three in scenes the alien can judge), never as a pass or fail. A wrong pick shows the misregistered stack.
3. `pictures.down` (answer `{ frame, quarterTurns }`): turn a picture on its side until it is upright; OK when the turn matches Barry's table (`orientationBarry`, our reading of his code: 1 = the native frame turned 90 degrees clockwise, 2 = anticlockwise; read `tools/ref-decoder/` and `docs/research/teaching-frames.md`). 4. `pictures.numerals` (answer `{ table: { [glyphIndex]: count } }`): on L002, beside each row of dots, the senders' digit (a crop from the decoded frame, `data/teaching.json`); the player builds a table from their digits to the Finder's own numerals (the codex's glyph numerals: if the game has none yet, add a small set of ten alien numeral glyphs to `src/game/glyphs.ts` in the same generator style, documented, and use them everywhere a count appears in the chapter). 5. `pictures.units` (answer `{ secondInBeats }`): on L003, the player reads "1 42/100 x 10^9 t = 1 s" with the table and turns it into beats with the instrument's converter (their t is the cover's spin flip); OK within the reading's own precision (three figures).
