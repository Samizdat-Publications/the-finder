## Performance targets (new; PLAN says "met" without numbers)

Measured in Playwright on this PC's Intel Arc 140T (the laptop stand-in, `--force_low_power_gpu
--use-angle=d3d11`) at 1440x900, after a 2 s settle, over 5 s of the scene animating:
- the encounter scene (Arrival, Portrait's sky), the galaxy chart and the neighbourhood chart: at most 25 ms per
  frame rendered back to back (the scenes' own `benchmark(120)`) and a live loop of at most 34 ms per frame (30 fps
  floor, with headless timer slack);
- entering any chapter: no long task over 100 ms (exists as `enter`; also run it on the iGPU);
- a picture decode in the Worker: under 150 ms per frame on the iGPU machine (CPU-bound; recorded, not gated).
The test records each number in `docs/checks/results.json` like every other check.

## Steps

1. **Clipped and stray things (quick fixes).** Scene and chart labels kept inside their canvas (flip to the
   other side of the anchor, or wrap, when they would cross the edge): Arrival's reading labels, the galaxy
   chart's Galactic centre label, any other `makeLabel`. Our Frame's second Sound button removed (the top bar
   has one). Portrait's "Their world" inner scroll at 1366x768 with the guide open. A browser check that no
   label's box leaves its canvas in the tour's stops.
2. **The tour opens on the spacecraft.** The prologue's story cards sit over the encounter scene (the craft
   under the computed sky, the camera drifting slowly), not a blank page with a line drawing; the cover
   drawing moves to the card where the disc is held up. Reduced motion: a still.
3. **One obvious control per step (layout pass).** In the tour, the instrument shows the step's own control
   and result at full strength; the other panels dim and the long readouts fold behind "show all". No "more
   below" on the step's own control at 1440x900 or 1366x768. Free play keeps everything open but gets the
   same folds. A layout test per tour stop: the spotlit control and the Propose button are inside the
   viewport without scrolling.
4. **Shorter words in the tour.** "What we just learned" at most two short sentences (the lead rewrites them;
   the long story stays in "The real story"); in the tour, the human gist of each number first ("about 400
   years") and the alien value after. Part dots labelled (the part's name on hover and under the current
   dot), so the 65 steps read as twelve parts.
5. **Art pass.** The galaxy and neighbourhood charts given depth (the Trail chart is flat): soft glow on the
   stars that matter, a faint grid in the alien's style, the region and trail drawn as light, not lines; the
   hub ring and instrument frames given the alien instrument look (radial ticks, glyph borders) without
   costing legibility; the finale's empty areas used (the record turning slowly while the Cavatina plays).
   Gemini concept art may steer it (log the spend); nothing generated stands in for record content.
6. **Laptop performance.** The iGPU perf test above; fix what misses (pixel ratio cap, star count by tier,
   no work in hidden canvases); warm the first WebGL context at boot so entering The Map has no long task.
7. **Ship pages.** The landing page with footage (a short film of the tour from `tools/harness/film-tour.mjs`,
   compressed under 20 MB, plus stills); the README in Stewart's style (badges, screenshots, plain words,
   honest sourcing, how to play, how it's checked); `docs/CREDITS.md` checked against every source in
   `tools/sources.json` and every asset; the How it's checked page read through once for the final counts.
8. **Leftovers.** The SI units still printed in Arrival and Key result messages and in a few readings; The
   Pictures' notebook needing an inner scroll at 1440x900; ring labels 9 to 12 px on phones; the planets'
   8 discrepancies traced to a citable 1970s table (a scout first; if no table is found, keep "cause
   UNVERIFIED").
9. **Gate.** `npm run check` green; tour bot 65 of 65 and the whole-game bot 44 of 44; the blind player critic
   on a fresh tour film (stills) and, if the credits are back, the Gemini video critic; a QA audit on the
   PROGRESS claims; deploy and live checks (`live-smoke`, `live-game`, `live-tour`).
