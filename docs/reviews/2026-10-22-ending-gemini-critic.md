# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This feels like a cross between *Heaven's Vault* and a high-end NASA data visualization dashboard. The moment the perspective flips from the alien's cold deduction to our human reality, accompanied by that haunting choral music, gave me actual chills. It is incredibly dense with information, but it treats me like an intelligent adult. If the whole game can maintain this level of atmospheric reverence for the Golden Record, I will devour it.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] The "Talk about it" prompts (00:18, 00:36). Who am I talking to? Is this a multiplayer game, a classroom study guide, or a rhetorical question? It breaks the immersion of being an alien researcher and makes it feel like a museum kiosk.
2. [hurts] The wall of text at 00:36 detailing Voyager's instruments. It is just a spreadsheet. For a game about the majesty of space, reading a static table of dates and power statuses is a sudden momentum killer.
3. [nitpick] The slider puzzle at 00:05 is almost *too* guided. "Drag the round marker under the clock bars... until all four bars overlap." It feels like I am just executing instructions rather than deducing a solution. 
4. [nitpick] The text density in the right-hand panel (00:14) is very high. "What we just learned" and "The Real Story" competing for attention right after a puzzle solve might cause players to just skim it.

## What is clear
- The UI hierarchy is fantastic. It is immediately obvious what is the alien's raw data (left side) and what is the human translation/context (right side). 
- The progress dots (00:00, top right) clearly communicate how far along I am in this specific sequence.

## What confuses
- At 00:02, the user is manipulating sliders before the prompt even appears on the right. It is unclear if they are solving a puzzle or just idly playing with the UI before the game tells them what to do.

## What is beautiful
- 00:26. The hard cut from the alien dashboard to the stark, modern digital clock of "our time." 
- 00:51. The visualization of the golden record's run-out groove etching. It feels incredibly intimate and human after looking at cold math.

## What is boring
- 00:36. The table of Voyager 1 and 2 instruments. It's cool data, but visually lifeless compared to the rest of the experience. 

## Where the sound helps, and where it hurts
- **Helps:** The alien UI sounds (00:00-00:13) are perfectly tactile and grounding. The transition to the choral music at 00:26 is breathtaking and anchors the emotional weight of the entire experience.
- **Hurts:** It doesn't hurt anywhere. The audio is the strongest element of this slice.

## The three changes that would most improve it
1. Remove or recontextualize the "Talk about it" boxes so they feel like a diegetic part of the game (e.g., a logbook entry) rather than a high school worksheet.
2. Give the Voyager instrument table (00:36) some visual flair - maybe a diagram of the probe with lights actually turning off, rather than just a text table.
3. Break up the text delivery in the right panel (00:14) so the "Real Story" reveals *after* I digest the "What we just learned," giving the player room to breathe.
