# Player Review

_The Gemini video critic (gemini-3.1-pro-preview) on the whole guided tour film of the V1.0 draft (455 s, with sound), run 2026-10-27. The critic writes as a player; quoted as returned except dashes. Lead decisions follow at the end._

## Gut reaction
I am completely obsessed with this premise. It's like *Heaven's Vault* or *Outer Wilds*, but applied to real-world astrophysics and the Voyager probe. It makes me feel like a genuine xenoarchaeologist, piecing together humanity from a cold, dead machine. But my god, it reads like a textbook right now. The UI is beautifully crisp, but there is so much text on screen that I feel like I'm taking a university physics exam instead of playing a game. The moments of discovery: tuning the raster to see the circle, hearing the record play: are pure magic, but they are buried under a mountain of reading.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The handholding in the "Your Turn" boxes. At 01:13, it literally tells me exactly how to set the dial and drop the needle. Where is the deduction? If this is a puzzle game, give me the tools and the diagram, and let me figure it out. (I see "Guided tour" in the top left, but if this is the core gameplay loop, it's not a game, it's an interactive essay).
2. [hurts] The text density is exhausting. Look at 04:27 ("Their numbers") or Still 3. It's a wall of text. It stops the momentum dead.
3. [hurts] "The Real Story" trivia boxes clutter the active puzzle-solving space. I want to solve the puzzle first, *then* read the cool Wikipedia-style facts about Carl Sagan and NASA.
4. [nitpick] The pulsar map screen (05:35) is visually intimidating. The UI panels overlap and cramp the screen, making the actual map feel secondary to the sliders and text boxes.
5. [nitpick] The UI needs more tactile, diegetic sound effects. Dragging sliders and clicking buttons feels a bit like a silent web page when there isn't active audio playing.

## Working, don't touch
- The interactive rasterizer (02:00 - 02:43). Tuning the line time and stagger to resolve the image from noise is a 10/10 mechanic. It is the best part of the video.
- The visual design language (neon green/yellow on deep space blue). It feels like a high-tech, alien laboratory.
- The "Our portrait of them" summary screen (06:57 / Still 4). It gives a fantastic, holistic sense of accomplishment, looking at everything we've deduced.

## What is clear
The ultimate goal (decode the record) and the relationship between the golden cover's etched diagrams and the interactive tools you are provided. The UI's use of color (yellow for the record, green for interactive elements/success) effectively guides the eye.

## What confuses
The math behind the units (04:37). The leap from hydrogen atom transitions to base-10 metrics (meters, kilograms) is a massive cognitive jump, and the UI throws a staggering amount of numbers and conversion factors at you all at once. It feels like a massive difficulty spike in comprehension.

## What is beautiful
The image decoding process (02:43). Watching the scrambled, staggered noise slowly align into a perfect circle, and then auto-developing into the 1977 photographs of Earth and humanity. Also, the zoom out on the star map (06:21) to find our Sun is a profound, humbling visual.

## What is boring
Reading the heavy paragraphs of text before being allowed to touch the puzzle. The pacing halts completely when a new screen loads and I have to read 300 words of background context before I can start twisting dials.

## Where the sound helps, and where it hurts
**Helps:** The audio puzzle (01:42) is the emotional core of the game. Hearing the raw, buzzing data noise shift and pitch down until it suddenly becomes human greetings and music is a goosebump-inducing moment.
**Hurts:** The lack of ambient audio or UI feedback during the heavy reading/deduction sections. It makes the game feel like a static website rather than a piece of software operating on an alien ship.

## The three changes that would most improve it
1. Hide all "The Real Story" historical trivia until *after* the player has successfully solved that specific screen's puzzle. Use it as a reward, not an obstacle.
2. Remove the explicit step-by-step instructions in the "Your Turn" boxes. Let the player look at the alien diagram and figure out how to use the provided tools to match it.
3. Break the massive text blocks into smaller, digestible chunks, or use diegetic tooltips (e.g., hovering over a part of the probe brings up a short scanning sentence, rather than a permanent textbook paragraph on the right).

   made); kept.
3. "The real story" before solving: already shown only after the step is confirmed; the film pressed "Do it for
   me" on every step, so it always saw it.
4. The Map screen cramped: the chart got depth (rings, halos on matched pulsars, the region as light) in M7 step 5b.
5. A silent interface: quiet press and slider sounds added (D-A40).
