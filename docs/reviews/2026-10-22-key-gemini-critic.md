# Player Review

## Gut reaction
The concept is incredible - you're literally playing the alien decoding the Voyager Golden Record using real math and logic. But right now, it feels more like an interactive textbook or a slick educational web app than a mystery game like *Return of the Obra Dinn*. The game spoon-feeds you the answers in a side panel rather than letting you have the "aha!" moment yourself. Plus, there is a constant, high-pitched ringing sound in the background that makes me want to rip my headphones off.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The background audio drone (entire video). It's a continuous, ear-piercing high frequency that sounds like a hearing test. I want ambient space hum or absolute vacuum silence, not a migraine.
2. [hurts] It feels like a multiple-choice quiz rather than a deduction game. The right panel (e.g., Frame 2, "WHAT WE JUST LEARNED") literally explains the answer to the puzzle on the left. I want to deduce the binary system myself, not read a summary of it.
3. [hurts] The interface is entirely human. With "Next" buttons, neat little status dots, and standard web typography, it shatters the illusion that I am an alien entity discovering this. Where is the diegetic interface?
4. [nitpick] Wall of text. Frame 2 (Step 10 of 58) throws massive blocks of scientific notation and paragraphs at the player all at once. It's visually exhausting.

## Working, don't touch
- The interactive lamp tool. Changing the light angle to cast shadows and read shallow etchings (Frame 1) is a brilliant, tactile mechanic.
- The grounding in real science. Using the actual hydrogen spin-flip data to calculate time and distance makes the eventual solution feel incredibly earned (conceptually, at least).

## What is clear
- How to manipulate the physical record. The lamp mechanic (00:00-00:04) is immediately intuitive, showing exactly how light reveals the shallow etchings.
- The progression system. The UI (00:06, 00:29) clearly highlights what has been "CONFIRMED" and what steps remain in the sequence.

## What confuses
- Why I am playing as an "alien" but using a UI that speaks perfect English and uses modern human web design paradigms (dropdowns, toggle buttons, standard pagination). 
- At 00:16, a massive table of numbers ("map line 1" through 14) appears. It is unclear if I am expected to manually verify these calculations or just nod and accept that the game did the math for me.

## What is beautiful
- The golden hue of the record itself and the dynamic shadows. Watching the etchings suddenly pop into sharp relief when the lamp slider is pulled low (00:02) is a genuinely satisfying visual reward.

## What is boring
- Reading the right-hand panel. From 00:07 onwards, the "THE REAL STORY" and "WHAT WE JUST LEARNED" boxes read like a Wikipedia article. It stops the gameplay dead so I can read a lecture.

## Where the sound helps, and where it hurts
- **Helps:** The subtle, dry clicks when selecting options or changing screens (00:06, 00:24, 00:29) feel precise and responsive.
- **Hurts:** The inescapable, high-pitched sinusoidal drone that plays continuously from 00:00 to 00:32. It creates immediate auditory fatigue and actively makes me want to close the browser.

## The three changes that would most improve it
1. Kill the high-pitched background drone immediately and replace it with low, rumbling space ambience or complete silence.
2. Remove the right-hand "tutorial/explanation" panel. Integrate the clues into the environment or a diegetic notebook so the player has to connect the dots between the hydrogen symbol and the binary strokes themselves.
3. Redesign the UI to feel less like a modern React/Vue web dashboard. It needs physical, tactile, or truly "alien" interaction methods to sell the fantasy.
