# Gemini video critic on the polished tour (M7 gate, 2026-10-27)

_gemini-3.1-pro-preview on the whole guided tour filmed after the M7 changes (642 s, with sound; review/inbox/2026-10-27T17-50-08-tour-film), about $0.28. Quoted as returned except dashes; lead decisions at the end._

## Gut reaction
This is like a playable Carl Sagan documentary, and I mean that as a massive compliment. The premise of reverse-engineering the Voyager Golden Record using real physics and math is incredibly nerdy, and the execution of the actual signal decoding is pure magic. However, right now it feels much more like an interactive textbook than a puzzle game; it holds my hand so tightly that I feel like a passenger rather than the alien scientist I was promised to be.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The hand-holding completely robs the player of epiphanies. At 07:47, the game literally tells you "Turn the dial under long line to the centre... read 0.2. Click a line number on the chart...". If I am playing a game for fans of *Outer Wilds* or *Obra Dinn*, I want to deduce this myself. 
2. [hurts] The "alien" framing contradicts the UI. The pitch says I am an alien, but I am using an interface with English text, Arabic numerals, and human standard graphs (01:00). It lacks the xenolinguistic mystery of *Chants of Sennaar* or *Heaven's Vault*.
3. [hurts] The Pulsar Map (07:40) gets visually overwhelming. When rotating the 3D map, the thin lines and numbers become a tangled web, making it hard to track which designation belongs to which line without clicking blindly.
4. [nitpick] The "Your Turn" boxes feel like homework prompts. The writing in these specific boxes is instructional rather than immersive, breaking the fourth wall of being a scientist making a discovery.
5. [nitpick] The voice analysis section (05:19) feels a bit tedious. Dragging across the spectrum to mark stretches feels like busywork compared to the mechanical satisfaction of tuning the image receiver.

## Working, don't touch
- The image decoding sequence (02:58 to 03:50). Adjusting the line time, locking the pulse, and fixing the stagger to resolve the circle is an incredible, tactile mechanic.
- The audio playback interface (02:00). Dropping the needle and hearing the raw, buzzing data of the images is haunting.
- The "Real Story" sidebars. They provide excellent historical context without getting in the way of the main interface.
- The final summary dashboard (09:31). Seeing all the deduced data compiled into a single "Portrait of them" is highly satisfying.

## Steam review one-liner
A beautiful, intensely nerdy interactive documentary that lets you play space archeologist with the Voyager Golden Record: just don't expect it to let you solve the puzzles on your own.

## What I want next
An "Expert Mode" that removes the explicit instructions in the "Your Turn" boxes and lets me actually use my brain to connect the clues on the record cover to the instruments.

***

## What is clear
The layout, the historical context, and the mechanical function of the UI are crystal clear. The dual-pane design (interactive element on the left, reading/instruction on the right) ensures you never lose track of what you are supposed to be looking at. The visual feedback when you get something right (like the green "CONFIRMED" stamps at 05:32) is unmistakable.

## What confuses
It is confusing how much agency I actually have as a player. Am I solving a puzzle, or am I just executing a script? For example, at 01:09, when setting the decay clock, can I get it wrong? Can I fail? The game feels so heavily guided that it creates a dissonance between the complex astrophysics being discussed and the simple "click the shiny button" actions required from the player.

## What is beautiful
The moment at 03:39 when the jagged, staggered lines suddenly snap into a perfect, clean circle. It is a brilliant visual representation of tuning a signal. Similarly, at 04:29, watching the grid of 115 pictures develop line-by-line all at once is a stunning payoff for the previous mechanical work.

## What is boring
Reading the long blocks of text before getting to touch the interactive bits. The pacing sometimes halts completely to deliver a textbook's worth of explanation (e.g., 01:21 through 01:59). The game needs to let the player play with the dials *while* learning, rather than reading a manual and then executing the steps.

## Where the sound helps, and where it hurts
**Helps:** The sound is absolutely vital during the record playback. Hearing the steady, abrasive buzz of the image data at 02:52 makes the abstract concept of "data on a record" feel tangible and real. The ambient space drone in the background perfectly sets a lonely, cosmic mood.
**Hurts:** The UI sound effects (the little clicks and blips when opening menus or clicking next) are a bit generic and sharp. They sound like a standard sci-fi UI soundpack and occasionally clash with the profound, authentic audio of the Voyager record itself.

## The three changes that would most improve it
1. **Remove the explicit instructions:** Change the "Your Turn" prompts from step-by-step tutorials into actual puzzles. Give me the tools, give me the cover diagram, and let me figure out that I need to measure the dish to find the wavelength.
2. **Diegetic UI framing:** If I am an alien, make the UI feel like an alien translation matrix. Even if the text is in English for the player's sake, the framing of the dashboard could feel more like xenotech deciphering a human artifact, rather than a human web app.
3. **Pace the reading:** Break up the dense text blocks. Let the player tinker with the interactive elements first, fail a bit, and *then* provide the context or hints if they get stuck, rather than front-loading the solution before they even touch the controls.
